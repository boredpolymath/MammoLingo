# Content Governance and Withdrawal Workflow

Last updated: November 6, 2026  
Owner: Bored Polymath Studios

## Responsibility

Bored Polymath Studios owns decisions about content corrections, source changes, and emergency content withdrawals for MammoLingo. This applies to glossary explanations, suggested questions, sources, disclaimers, and legal notices.

MammoLingo currently has no public support or feedback channel. Potential issues may be identified through the owner’s scheduled reviews, source-publisher updates, internal testing, legal notices received through channels outside the app, or other credible information brought to the owner’s attention.

## Triage

When an issue is identified, record the date, affected entry or page, source of the issue, observed concern, and disposition. Classify it as:

- **Emergency:** potentially unsafe clinical implication, a substantiated copyright/trademark concern, exposure of personal information, or a material security issue.
- **High priority:** materially misleading or outdated information, broken key source link, or accessibility barrier.
- **Routine:** wording improvement, noncritical source update, typo, or presentation improvement.

## Response

For an emergency, Bored Polymath Studios will promptly remove or disable the affected content, or restrict access to the app if that is the safer option. Do not wait for a normal release cycle. Document the action, then investigate and publish a corrected version only after review.

For high-priority and routine issues, update the independently written copy or source information, run the relevant checks, publish the revision, and record the result in the content-change log.

## Required change record

For every substantive change, retain:

- entry or page affected;
- issue classification and rationale;
- source URL(s) and source-review date;
- editor/approver: Bored Polymath Studios;
- before/after summary;
- publication date and deployed version; and
- next-review date when applicable.

## Guardrails

Do not add patient-specific interpretations, recommendations, or report analysis through a correction. Escalate to qualified clinical and legal reviewers before a change that relies on proprietary standard content, expands medical scope, or introduces a new data flow.
